How to use Stamp Page Designer

Version 6.0.0. This guide follows the tool in the order you meet it: first screen, then the editor, then preview and printing. It is deliberately short — the tool is meant to be used, not studied.

Contents 1. The first screen 2. The editor, top to bottom 3. Stamps, headers, and description boxes 4. Page Settings 5. Preview, printing, and saving 6. Warnings, undo, and shortcuts 7. Where your work lives

1. The first screen

Every time you open the tool, you land on the first screen. Nothing opens automatically.

  • Continue your last work — opens the project you were last working on, with its pages, zoom, selection, and undo history.
  • Saved albums (up to 3) — buttons showing name and date only. Clicking one loads the full album. Saving a fourth album asks which slot to replace — it never silently deletes one, and never creates a fourth.
  • Saved settings (up to 3) — saved combinations of paper, margins, punch-hole, spacing, caption font, and page border. No stamps, no pages. Applied in one step.
  • Start fresh — starts a brand new project. See §2 below for its steps.

2. The editor, top to bottom

Start fresh, step by step

  1. Save first? — “Do you want to save your current work as an album first?” Choose Save it first, Don't save, or Cancel. Cancel leaves your current project completely unchanged.
  2. If you chose Don't save — a “Discard unsaved work?” confirm appears. Discard goes forward; Cancel goes back unchanged.
  3. Name — give the new project a name.
  4. Settings — pick a saved settings combination, or Default settings, if saved settings exist.
  5. Clean page — a new page, 1 page, 0 stamps, with those settings applied. Nothing is carried over from the old project.

The view bar

  • Add — add a stamp, header, subheader, description box, triangle, corner picture (Art), or row divider (Divider) to the page.
  • Auto-align — tidies the page onto the 2.5 mm grid, respecting your spacing, and never switches to Tight by itself. Every item's left and top edge lands on a visible 2.5 mm line. It leaves row dividers and corner pictures where you put them. If a page is too full, it falls back to Dense-style packing rather than failing.
  • Snap to neighbours — while dragging, an item's edge or centre within about 1 mm of a neighbour's edge or centre, or the page centre, pulls onto that line, with a pink alignment line shown on screen only. It can also snap to exactly your chosen spacing from a neighbour, with the blue gap measurement shown. Keep dragging past it to break free, or hold Alt (Option on Mac) to turn neighbour snapping off for that drag. Arrow keys never snap to neighbours, and snapping changes position only, never size.
  • Select All — selects everything on the current page.
  • Copy / Paste — copies the selected items. Paste puts them in the first free space, and pans into view if they would land off-screen. Copy is session-only: it does not survive closing and reopening the tool.
  • Start fresh — as above, from inside the editor.
  • Zoom, Fit to page, Fit to width — zoom changes only your view, never the printed size. Fit to page shows the whole page; Fit to width fills the window width.
  • Undo / Redo — up to 10 steps. One Undo reverts a whole applied settings combination, and one Undo reverts a whole group move.

3. Stamps, headers, and description boxes

Stamp boxes

  • Sizes: Small 25 × 25 mm, Medium 30 × 30 mm, Large 35 × 35 mm, Extra Large 40 × 40 mm. Custom stamps can be any rectangle, up to 210 × 210 mm, which fits a plate block. A stamp that would go beyond the usable area stops at the edge.
  • Moving: drag any item from anywhere on it — the box, its border, or its caption. The pointer shows a hand. Visible grid lines stay at 2.5 mm, moving and resizing snap to 1.25 mm, and faint 1.25 mm lines appear at 100% zoom and above only (screen only, never in Preview or PDF). A plain arrow key jumps to the next visible 2.5 mm line; Shift + arrow moves 1.25 mm.
  • Resizing: Small, Medium, Large, Extra Large and Custom stamps all resize with the mouse in 2.5 mm steps, growing and shrinking, with the caption strip moving with the box — a preset size is a starting point, and resizing it turns it into Custom (dragging back to exactly 30 × 30 mm makes it Medium again). Description boxes resize from the bottom-right and top-right handles, both snapping width and height to 2.5 mm.
  • Captions: a 5 mm strip directly below each stamp, up to 40 characters. Caption font is set per page — Source Sans 3, 9 pt by default, maximum 10 pt, from 18 fonts in total (see below). The caption panel appears only when a stamp is selected, not when a header is selected. Script fonts can be hard to read at caption size — the tool shows a hint, but never blocks the choice.
  • Fonts: 18 fonts in total, available in every text control including captions, grouped as Script, Handwriting, Sans-serif, Serif, and Typewriter / code, each shown in its own typeface with its style label. New in 6.0.0: Great Vibes, Italianno, Kaushan Script, Kalam, Cabin Sketch, Open Sans, Nunito Sans, Roboto, Cormorant Garamond, and Source Code Pro. Bold and Italic are greyed out (disabled) where a font does not have them — script fonts have neither, Kalam and Cabin Sketch have Bold only. Underline always works. Switching to a font without Bold or Italic turns that style off, and switching back does not restore it automatically. All fonts are built into the single tool file, so they print correctly with no internet connection.
  • S-numbers: each stamp gets an S-number in the order it was added on that page. S-numbers are never reused after a stamp is removed, and they travel with the stamp on drag, copy, undo, and duplicate.
  • Mounts: Standard mount clearance 2.5 mm per side, Strips 5 mm per side, measured from the box edge. Spacing between items is shared once, border to border, never double-counted: Tight 5 mm, Narrow 10 mm, Normal 20 mm, Wide 30 mm. Spacing is set separately across (between columns) and down (between rows), and row spacing is measured below a stamp's caption, never between a stamp and its own caption.
  • Corner pictures (Art): 23 pictures to personalise a page — 9 philatelic, 6 postal, 8 heraldic — starting as Crown at 30 × 30 mm. Each picture sits in a box on the grid, resizes with the blue corners (minimum 10 mm a side, drawing scales without stretching), and takes the page-border colour, following it when the border colour changes. Heraldic shields only: supporters (None, Two lions, Two unicorns) and a scroll ribbon with a motto. Pictures warn only on overlap, never block printing, and Auto-align leaves them where you put them. Generic heraldry only — not any country's official arms.
  • Row dividers (Divider): a horizontal line separating sections of a page, full usable width and centred by default, in the page-border colour. It moves up and down only. Styles: single, double, double wavy, dotted, thick-and-thin, and short centred line with an ornament (any border motif). Weights: Thin, Medium, Thick. Width: full, or Narrower (shorter, still centred). Dividers warn only on overlap, never block printing, and Auto-align leaves them where you put them.
  • Resize handles: the two blue corners appear only on the stamp you have selected — unselected stamps show no handles, so the page stays clean.
  • Triangle (trial): one point-up triangle tile, with Base and Height inputs, a 2 mm border, and no mount. Its footprint is taken from its bounding rectangle. It also resizes with the mouse, like the other stamps.

Headers and subheaders

  • Page Header: optional, one per page, centred, heights 25 / 30 / 35 / 40 mm. A second Page Header on the same page is refused.
  • Subheaders: up to 2 per page, heights 15 / 20 / 25 mm.
  • Moving headers: Page Headers and Subheaders move up and down only — never sideways, singly or in a group move. In a group move, stamps and description boxes move sideways and headers stay put; moving down moves everything.

Description boxes

  • Centred text, width 30–200 mm, height 10–200 mm. Text size up to 30 pt, for larger print — this is separate from caption font, which stays at 9 pt. Resize from the bottom-right or top-right blue corners, both snapping to 2.5 mm.
  • Description boxes are the exception to the spacing warnings: they may sit tight against a stamp, a heading, or the edge of the usable area with no warning — only a real overlap is reported.
  • Plain click on one item inside a multi-selection selects only that item, like other tools. Dragging still moves the whole group. Clicking empty page space clears the selection. Shift-click and marquee drag also select multiple items.

4. Page Settings

Page Settings is a permanent panel under the selected-item panel. It is album-wide, and its open/closed state is remembered.

  • Paper: Letter, A4, Legal, A3, plus Scott National & Specialty, Scott International, Scott Minuteman, Minkus, Davo, H. E. Harris, Stanley Gibbons, Palo, Lighthouse, White Ace, Mystic Heirloom and Schaubek — portrait only. No landscape.
  • Margins: Classic (about 1 inch at the sides, trimmed so the usable height divides by 5 mm) or Dense (wider usable area). With punch-hole on, the left margin grows by ½ inch, punch-hole side only, on every format.
  • Page border: 23 patterns, 17 corners, 8 colours each, in Small (about 0.75×), Medium (today's size), or Large (about 1.4×), default grey / hyphens / square. Decorations always sit outside the usable area and clear of the 4 mm strip most printers cannot reach. In Dense margins, if Large does not fit outside the line, it either shrinks to the largest size that fits or moves onto the line, and the panel states which. An album without a border size saved opens at Medium, exactly today's size. There is also a None choice — with None, the usable area is unchanged and page numbers, when on, sit just under the usable area, centred.
  • Page numbers: off by default — you must turn them on. When on, the starting number defaults to 1 and can be changed. Numbers follow page order and update when you add, remove, or reorder pages. They print on the PDF.
  • Saved settings: save the current combination from Page Settings. Applying a saved combination is one action, and one Undo reverts it. Settings and albums are separate — saving one never overwrites the other.
  • New pages start with the album's original setup, not with a changed paper on the current page.

Working colours on screen only, never printed: header pale yellow, subheader pale blue, stamps pale green, description pale grey. The grid, working colours, and S-numbers are on screen only in the editor — see Preview.

5. Preview, printing, and saving

  • Preview hides the grid, working colours, and warnings, and keeps S-numbers. What you see in Preview is the layout that prints.
  • Print / Save as PDF: print at 100% only, never “Fit to page”. At 100%, a 40 mm stamp prints at 40 mm. Check your printer's scale setting before printing a real page.
  • Save album saves the full editable project — paper, margins, spacing, punch-hole, border, caption font, all pages, stamp sizes and positions, S-numbers, subheaders, description boxes, and triangles — into one of the 3 album slots.
  • Save project ≠ PDF. Save keeps the editable project in the browser. Print / Save as PDF is output only, and a PDF cannot be reopened as a project.

6. Warnings, undo, and shortcuts

  • Warnings appear as a folded line on the page, counted by kind of problem and by item — for example, “4 items overlap or sit closer than the spacing”. Opening the line lists each item once, with its clash partners and the pair count. A count can rise fast on a full page because each stamp in a tight grid can clash with up to 8 neighbours; that is not double-counting — each line is a different pair.
  • Genuine overlaps do not print: a stamp overlapping the page border, or a stamp overlapping another stamp, is not printed — instead you are returned to the editor on that page to fix it, with the overlap shown. Closeness alone, without overlapping, shows the red warning but does not block printing. Corner pictures and row dividers never block printing — they show an overlap warning only.
  • Saved work from 5.1.0 opens unchanged: saved albums, saved settings, and Continue work from 5.1.0 open with all pages, sizes, positions, S-numbers, borders, fonts, colours, and backgrounds exactly as saved, and nothing is auto-fixed. Today's border size reads as Medium.
  • Duplicate Page creates a copy immediately after the original, with everything duplicated including layout and numbering per page.
  • Working colours, grid, and S-numbers never print. Colours, grids, and S-numbers never print; the on-screen view carries grids, colours, and S-numbers; Preview carries S-numbers only; the PDF removes grids.
  • No thumbnails, no margin text, no crop marks, no landscape, no circle or pentagon shapes — by design, to keep the tool simple and the pages clean.

7. Where your work lives

Your albums live in this browser, on this device. There is no account, no cloud copy, and no project file to download or open. If browser storage is cleared, saved albums are lost. The printed PDF is your visual archive — keep it, and you can rebuild a page from it quickly.

Continue your last work is a separate, automatically kept slot, distinct from the three named albums. It is overwritten as you work.
